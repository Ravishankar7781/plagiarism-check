Bees on the Roof

My uncle started keeping bees on his flat roof two summers ago, and I've been helping him ever since. Honeybees kept on city roofs often produce more honey than hives in the countryside, because parks, street trees and balcony gardens offer a long and varied flowering season. That surprised me at first.

But it isn't all good news. Managed honeybees compete with wild bees for the same limited pollen and nectar supply when a district has too many hives. Some ecologists say cities should plant more flowers before they add more hives.

In today's fast-paced world, urban beekeeping plays a crucial role in fostering a deeper connection between communities and nature. Furthermore, it is important to note that beekeeping serves as a testament to the multifaceted relationship between humans and the environment. Moreover, it offers a myriad of benefits, including education, sustainability, and community engagement. Additionally, beekeepers must navigate the complexities of regulations, safety, and colony health.

Honestly, the hardest part is the stings. I got three last week. We still check the hive every Saturday though, and the honey we got in August was dark and tasted a bit like mint.
